# 2510.15205: Toward Black-Scholes for Prediction Markets

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Toward Black-Scholes for Prediction Markets: A Unified Kernel and Market-Maker's Handbook
- **Authors:** Shaw Dalen; Daedalus Research Team
- **arXiv:** 2510.15205v2 [cs.CE]. v2 is dated 6 Apr 2026, and the references show access dates of Oct 9, 2025, so v1 is from about Oct 2025.
- **Length:** 25 pages: 20 of body and 5 of references. The text points to an appendix (p.17, footnote 2), but no appendix is included.

## 2. Summary

The paper models a binary event-contract price p_t as a martingale under the risk-neutral measure Q. It takes log-odds x = logit(p) and treats them as a jump-diffusion. The martingale condition fixes the drift, so belief volatility sigma_b, jump intensity and jump moments, and cross-event correlation and co-jumps are what remain as quotable risk factors (pp.6-7). On that kernel it builds a derivative layer (belief-variance swaps, correlation swaps, corridor variance, first-passage notes; p.8) and a market-maker handbook: Greeks in logit units, Avellaneda-Stoikov quoting in logit space, and hedging and kill-switch rules (pp.9-13). It also gives a calibration pipeline: a heteroskedastic Kalman filter, then EM to separate diffusion from jumps, then RN-drift enforcement, then spline smoothing over (time-to-resolution, moneyness) (pp.13-16). The single experiment forecasts 60-second realized logit variance and compares against random-walk, constant-sigma, Wright-Fisher, and AR-GARCH baselines (pp.17-20). It does **not** forecast event outcomes, and it does not measure calibration of probabilities against resolution.

## 3. Data and method

- **Kernel:** dx_t = mu dt + sigma_b dW + compensated jumps (Eq.1, p.6). Drift is pinned by Eq.3 (p.7). Instantaneous covariance across events is diffusive plus co-jump terms (Eq.4, p.7).
- **Observation model:** y_t = logit(p~_t) = x_t + eta_t. Noise variance is sigma_eta^2 = a0 + a1*s^2 + a2/d + a3*r + a4*iota^2, where s is spread, d is depth, r is trade rate, and iota is aggressor imbalance (Eq.10, p.14).
- **Canonical mid:** a trade-weighted mid whose weights increase with trade size and inverse spread. Updates smaller than one tick are ignored. p is clamped to [eps, 1-eps] with eps = 1e-5, then resampled to a 100 ms to 1 s grid (p.13).
- **EM jump/diffusion split:** posterior jump probability gamma_t; an interval counts as jump-dominant when gamma_t > tau_J, for example 0.7 (p.14). M-step updates are in Eqs.11-12 (p.15).
- **Experiment data:** this is internally inconsistent. Section 6.2 says "20 high-volume event trades from Polymarket" with added synthetic heteroskedastic noise (p.17). Table 1 and the conclusion describe a "synthetic RN-consistent path" (pp.19-20). The abstract claims "real event data" (p.1). Settings: N = 6000 steps at 1 Hz (p.19); split into train, validation, and test thirds shared by all methods (p.18).
- **Causality:** all models are causal, and the last h timestamps are dropped so forward sums cannot leak (p.17, fn.2; p.19). Known announcement windows enter as ex-ante Gaussian kernels (p.18).

## 4. Key quantitative findings (exact values)

Table 1 (p.19) reports causal H = 60 s forward-sum forecasts of realized logit variance:

| Model | MSE_all | MAE_all | QLIKE_all |
|---|---|---|---|
| RN-JD (causal path) | 70.281 | 1.588 | 1.4621 |
| RW-logit (const sigma) | 77.414 | 1.163 | 4.7318 |
| Logit (const sigma) | 76.752 | 2.078 | 2.6594 |
| WF/Jacobi (mapped) | 1.71e17 | 3.67e7 | 1.9484 |
| ARMA-GARCH (mapped) | 1.07e19 | 5.33e8 | 0.7962 |

- The text says RN-JD "achieves the lowest overall MSE, MAE, and logMSE" (p.19). **That is not what the table shows.** RW-logit has the lower MAE (1.163 against 1.588), even though the table bolds 1.588. ARMA-GARCH has the best QLIKE (0.7962 against 1.4621). No logMSE column is reported.
- The text gives RN-JD as MSE_all = 70.28 and QLIKE_all = 1.46 (p.19).
- Implementation (p.19): 6 global EM steps, then a rolling EM window of 400 s; 600 Monte Carlo draws per step for the drift; schedule kernels 90 s wide; the jump boost capped at the 95th percentile of smoothed lambda; c_J grid-searched over {0.3, ..., 1.0} by QLIKE on the validation slice.
- Drift is EWMA-smoothed and capped at |0.25| per second; S'(x) is clipped at 1e-4 (p.18, fn.3).
- Handbook constants: cross-event beta shrinkage alpha in [0.5, 1) (p.11); a refresh loop of 100-500 ms (p.11); a day-one corridor variance on p in [0.35, 0.65] (p.13).
- Context figures cited from press reports: Polymarket about $1.4B and Kalshi $1-3B monthly volume, Sept 2025 (p.4). ICE up to $2B investment, roughly 20% stake, about $8B pre-money (p.3).

## 5. Microstructure, calibration, fees, biases, LLMs, leakage

- **Microstructure:** Delta_x = p(1-p) and Gamma_x = p(1-p)(1-2p). Curvature peaks in the "swing zone" near p = 0.5 (p.9). Order flow is called most toxic in the swing zone (p.6). Spreads and cancellations widen around scheduled news, and quotes gap on unscheduled announcements (p.6). Probability-space spreads compress near 0 and 1 because delta_p is about p(1-p)*delta_x (p.10). Pinned markets near 0 or 1 have tiny p-variance even when x-variance is large (p.16).
- **Toxicity:** order imbalance and VPIN-style spikes trigger wider quotes or pulled quotes. Quotes pre-widen before scheduled news and pause on unscheduled jump alarms (pp.10, 12).
- **Fees:** mentioned once. "Target stable edge per fill after fees and expected adverse selection" (p.12). There is no fee model.
- **Calibration:** "calibration" here means fitting the vol surface. The paper reports no Brier score, reliability analysis, or probability-versus-outcome tests. It assumes price equals the risk-neutral probability, and notes that "biases exist and interpretation requires care" (p.2, citing Wolfers-Zitzewitz and Manski).
- **Favorite-longshot bias:** not analyzed.
- **LLM forecasting and retrieval:** not covered.
- **Leakage:** the controls are a causal forward-sum, dropping the last h timestamps, a shared train/val/test split, and schedule information restricted to what is "known ex-ante" (pp.17-19).

## 6. Implications for our system

**Backtest variations**

1. **Work in logit space.** Measure the forecast-minus-market edge as delta-x rather than delta-p. Check whether the edge after fees, conditional on |delta-x|, is more stable across price buckets than delta-p is. This bears directly on favorite-longshot tests, because the p(1-p) Jacobian (p.9) mechanically shrinks moves in the tails.
2. **Swing-zone versus tails split.** Stratify calibration and PnL by p in [0.35, 0.65] versus the tails (p.13). Under the paper's view, toxic flow concentrates in the swing zone (p.6), so model edge should net out lower there after adverse selection.
3. **Scheduled-event windows.** Tag debates, primaries, poll releases, and FEC deadlines as ex-ante windows (p.18). Compare model-versus-market Brier inside and outside those windows. Test the rule of not trading, or requiring a larger edge, within a set number of minutes before a scheduled release (the news guard, p.10).
4. **Jump-conditioned evaluation.** Use the EM posterior gamma_t > 0.7 (p.14) to flag jump intervals in Kalshi price history. Report calibration separately for forecasts made before a jump and after it is absorbed. This tests whether our knowledge files lag news.
5. **Volatility-aware edge threshold.** Require |model - market| to exceed a multiple of the market's forecasted short-horizon sigma_b*p(1-p), plus fees. Then check whether the hit rate improves over a fixed-cents threshold.
6. **Paper replication sanity check.** If we build the variance forecaster, include RW-logit and AR-GARCH baselines and report MAE and QLIKE honestly. The paper's own table shows simple baselines winning on those metrics (p.19).

**Retrieval design.** The paper says nothing directly about retrieval. By analogy, each markdown knowledge file should carry an "as-of" timestamp and a "scheduled catalysts" list, the counterpart of the paper's ex-ante schedule kernels (p.18), so the retriever can mark when a fact is likely to be stale.

**Leakage and lookahead controls**

- Use a strict forward-sum rule: drop the last h observations before resolution (p.17, fn.2).
- Fit all hyperparameters (thresholds, shrinkage, fee buffer) on a validation slice that is separate from training and test (p.18).
- Event schedules may enter only if they were published before the decision time.
- Any filtered or smoothed mid has to be causal. A Kalman *smoother* (p.14) uses future data and must not feed live features.

**Participant-sophistication features (from Eq.10, p.14, and pp.10-13)**

- Spread s_t, depth d_t, trade rate r_t, and aggressor imbalance iota_t, as inputs to a fitted microstructure-noise variance. A high fitted noise share suggests a less informed or less efficient market.
- Weight trades by size times inverse spread when building the canonical mid (p.13).
- Reaction speed: the lag between a flagged jump (gamma_t > tau_J) and the moment the filtered x stabilizes. VPIN or order-imbalance spikes before jumps can serve as an informed-flow proxy (p.10).
- Bid/ask flicker below one tick, and crossed or locked books, as quality flags (p.13).

## 7. Limitations and caveats

- The paper does not forecast outcomes or test probability calibration, fees, or the favorite-longshot bias. Its relevance to beating the market price is indirect.
- The data description contradicts itself: the abstract says real data, Section 6.2 says Polymarket trades plus synthetic noise, and Table 1 and Section 7 say a synthetic path (pp.1, 17, 19-20). Treat the results as synthetic.
- It evaluates one experiment and one 60 s horizon, gives no confidence intervals or significance tests, and the referenced appendix with bi-power robustness checks is missing (p.17).
- Its claim of lowest MAE and logMSE does not match Table 1 (p.19).
- Its own stated limitations (pp.20-21): single-event dynamics only, synthetic co-jumps, symmetric light-tailed jumps, and stylized, venue-agnostic microstructure. Kalshi has nothing to do with this work beyond the volume figures cited from press reports.
- The handbook constants (alpha, tau_J, eps, corridor bounds) are heuristics given "e.g.", not values the paper validated empirically.
