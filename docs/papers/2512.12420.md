# 2512.12420: Deep Hedging with Reinforcement Learning

**Relevance warning:** This paper is **not** about prediction markets, Kalshi, calibration, the favorite-longshot bias, or LLM forecasting. It trains an RL agent to hedge SPX/SPY option exposure. What carries over to our system is its methodology: leakage controls, cost-aware evaluation, cost/cadence sweeps, Sharpe confidence intervals, regime attribution and robustness checks. Skim the rest.

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Deep Hedging with Reinforcement Learning: A Practical Framework for Option Risk Management
- **Authors:** Travon Lucius, Christian (Chip) Koch Jr, Jacob Starling, Julia Zhu, Miguel Urena, Carrie Hu (affiliations per emails: BlackRock, Emory; disclaimer p.9 says the work is independent of both)
- **Date:** November 2025 on the title page; arXiv v1 posted 13 Dec 2025
- **arXiv:** 2512.12420v1 [q-fin.PM]
- **Code:** https://github.com/tlucius16/deep-hedging-rl (p.1)
- 9 pages.

## 2. Summary

The authors train a small stochastic actor-critic agent (GAE, two-layer 256-unit MLP, squashed-Gaussian actions) to set a hedge level in SPY against one unit of option-implied equity exposure. Its inputs are daily end-of-day option-surface and macro features: ATM IV at 30d/91d, term slope, 25-delta skew, VIX, the 10Y yield, and realized/historical vol. The reward is per-step PnL in basis points after proportional transaction costs. They use a fixed date split (train ≤2017-12-31, validation 2018–2019, test ≥2020-01-01) and run a sweep over rebalance cadence and slippage. On test, the policy gets Sharpe 0.502. It is the only policy whose 95% CI excludes zero, but that CI overlaps long-SPY, so the authors explicitly stop short of claiming dominance (pp.1, 6, 8). A 50/50 blend with long SPY gets test Sharpe 0.65 (p.7).

## 3. Data & method

- **Panel:** daily SPX/SPY from 2005 onward. The cleaned panel has 18,018 trading days, and IV fields are populated on ~96% of rows. The rest are dropped by spread/delta filters and treated as missing (p.3).
- **Features:** iv_atm_30d, iv_atm_91d, slope (iv_91d − iv_30d), 25d put/call IV and skew (IV_25d,put − IV_25d,call, mid-quote, 30d tenor), VIX, rate_10y, rv_21d, hvol_30d, hvol_91d (p.3). Option quotes are chosen by tenor/delta tolerance with "spread-aware tie-breaking" that favors liquid quotes (p.3).
- **Staleness guard:** IV series forward-fill at most 2 calendar days, after which they are treated as missing (`ffill_limit=2`, p.3).
- **Target:** R_t = (P_{t+1} − P_t)/P_t, shifted forward one step so the agent cannot see realized PnL before it acts (p.3).
- **Normalization:** observations are W×F windows (window=30) normalized with statistics from the **training split only** (p.3). NaN/inf values are replaced with 0 to prevent accidental leakage (p.3).
- **Environment:** actions a_t ∈ [−a_max, +a_max]; cost c_t = κ|a_t − a_{t−1}|; p_t = a_t R_t − c_t; reward r_t = 10⁴ p_t (p.3). There is an optional Almgren–Chriss-style slippage hook (spread φ, depth ψ, permanent impact λ), but the reported runs set ψ=0 and λ=0 (p.4).
- **RL:** entropy-regularized policy gradient with GAE, γ=0.99, grad clip 1.0, cosine LR, entropy coefficient 0.01. Checkpoints are evaluated every 50 updates and the one with the best validation Sharpe is kept (p.4). Training snippet: lr=5e-4, steps=2500 (p.5).
- **Baselines:** no-hedge, momentum, volatility-targeting, buy-and-hold SPY, a VIX-band rule, a VIX vol-target rule, and an ML surrogate (ml_gbr). Delta hedges "are dominated" and are only summarized qualitatively (pp.4–6).
- **Metrics:** annualized Sharpe (×√252), max drawdown, turnover Σ|Δposition|, hit-rate, cost-normalized profit (pp.4–5).
- **Significance:** Newey–West SEs with a 21-day lag, plus 95% block-bootstrap intervals on the test split (p.6).

## 5. Relevance to our topics

- **Prediction-market microstructure / calibration / FLB / LLM forecasting / retrieval:** none. The paper does not touch them.
- **Fees/frictions:** it puts proportional costs, cadence limits and fixed slippage directly into the reward, and it chose the config by robustness of test Sharpe under cost stress (p.5). Its stated rationale is that slower rebalancing cuts churn "without sacrificing much return" (pp.5–6).
- **Leakage controls (the most transferable part):** the forward-shifted target, train-only scaler statistics, capped forward-fill of stale quotes, deterministic date splits, and versioned artifacts and configs (pp.2–3). It also has pytest invariants for shape, NaN guards and deterministic rollouts (p.8).
- **Liquidity-aware feature construction:** spread-aware tie-breaking that prefers liquid quotes, and spread/delta filters that drop ~4% of rows (p.3).

## 6. Implications for our Kalshi system

**Backtest variations to run:**
1. **Fee × cadence grid.** Mirror the {cadence} × {slippage} sweep (p.5). Grid our edge threshold, rebalance/re-forecast cadence and an extra slippage (in cents) on top of Kalshi fees. Report Brier/log-loss *and* post-fee PnL for each cell, and pick the most robust cell, not the best one.
2. **Regime attribution.** The paper's "VIX terciles" (p.6) map to our terciles of market volume, days-to-resolution, or news intensity. Report model-minus-market Brier per tercile. The paper's edge was concentrated in high-vol regimes, so check whether ours is concentrated in thin or fast-moving markets.
3. **Named-period table.** Make an equivalent of Table 3 (p.6) by election cycle (2022 midterms, 2024 primaries, 2024 general, specials) with Δ = model − market. Expect some negative cells. The paper had Δ −1.01 in a calm train period.
4. **Uncertainty on the edge.** Use block bootstrap over *events*, not contracts, because many contracts resolve on the same night and are correlated. This is analogous to their Newey–West plus block bootstrap (p.6). Claim an edge only if the CI on (market Brier − model Brier) excludes zero. The paper is honest that its CI overlaps the benchmark (pp.6, 8–9), and we should be equally strict.
5. **Shifted-split robustness.** Re-run with the train/test boundary moved by one cycle, and with multiple seeds or prompt variants for any LLM component. Report mean ± std as on p.8.
6. **Blend with market.** Their 50/50 overlay beat either leg on Sharpe (p.7). Test p_final = w·p_model + (1−w)·p_market over a w-sweep, like their Fig. 5 frontier (p.7). This is likely the right production form for calibration.

**Leakage/lookahead controls:**
- Shift every label so features carry a timestamp strictly before the decision time (p.3). Every knowledge-file chunk gets an `as_of` timestamp, and retrieval filters to `as_of < decision_ts`.
- Fit all scalers, calibrators (Platt/isotonic) and FLB corrections on train only (p.3).
- Cap staleness. Set a forward-fill limit on last-trade/orderbook features (their limit is 2 days, p.3) and mark the feature missing after it, not stale-filled.
- Replace NaN deterministically and add a missingness flag (p.3). Write pytest invariants for no-future-timestamp, deterministic replay and shape (p.8).
- Version the config, data snapshot and knowledge-base snapshot per run (p.3).

**Participant-sophistication features:**
- Their "spread-aware tie-breaking" and quote filters (p.3) suggest weighting or filtering market prices by bid-ask spread and depth before using price as a feature or benchmark. Wide-spread mid-prices are a noisier "market belief".
- The optional linear-quadratic impact model (spread φ, depth ψ, permanent λ; p.4) can serve as a cost model for sizing trades against Kalshi book depth. Do not reuse their ψ=λ=0 shortcut for thin political books.

**Retrieval design:** no direct lessons. The paper's general preference for compact, interpretable engineered features over latent embeddings (pp.1, 4) argues for structured, auditable knowledge-file fields (polls, fundamentals, timestamps) that can be traced per forecast.

## 7. Limitations / caveats

- Different domain (equity options hedging). It has no evidence on prediction markets, calibration or LLMs.
- Its test Sharpe CI overlaps long-SPY, and Sharpe differentials are not formally tested (pp.6, 8–9).
- The config was picked by test-Sharpe robustness during the sweep (p.5), which risks selection on test data.
- Daily bars only, with no intraday execution (p.8). Nonlinear impact is disabled in the reported runs (p.4).
- Only three seeds, with no exhaustive architecture search or extra walk-forwards (p.8).
- There are inconsistencies between the cost text and the code (10 bps vs `txn_cost_bps=1.0`), and the doubled-cost robustness claim is not shown in a table (pp.1, 4–5).
- Overlapping windowed episodes inflate the step counts (p.5), so the effective sample size is smaller than the step counts suggest.
