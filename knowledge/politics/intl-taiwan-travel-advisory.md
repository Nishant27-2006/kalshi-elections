# International: Taiwan Travel Advisory Level

Back to [[Politics Knowledge Index]]

Scope: the State Department's travel advisory level for Taiwan, its history since the four-level system began, nearby comparators, and Kalshi pricing for a Level 4 ("Do Not Travel") before 2027-2030.

## Claims

- In a January 2018 archived copy, the State Department's Taiwan page showed "Level 1: Exercise normal precautions." (as_of: 2018-01-12; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20180112181213/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- In May 2020 the Taiwan page itself stayed at Level 1, under a separate Global Health Advisory telling Americans to avoid all international travel because of COVID-19 ("Do Not Travel"). (as_of: 2020-05-10; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20200510142129/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- By April 2021 Taiwan was at "Level 3: Reconsider Travel" because of COVID-19-related travel restrictions, while the CDC rated Taiwan's COVID-19 level as low. (as_of: 2021-04-22; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20210422030747/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- At the end of August 2022 Taiwan was still at Level 3, citing COVID-19 restrictions and a CDC "high" COVID-19 level. The stated reason was health, not security. (as_of: 2022-08-31; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20220831194830/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- By late December 2022 Taiwan was back at "Level 1: Exercise Normal Precautions," reissued with updated health information. (as_of: 2022-12-29; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20221229080310/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- In September 2024 Taiwan was at Level 1, "Reissued after periodic review with minor edits," with a July 31, 2024 date on the page. (as_of: 2024-09-09; source: http://web.archive.org/web/20240909172842/https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/taiwan-travel-advisory.html)
- The current Taiwan advisory, published 24 November 2025, is "Level 1: Exercise Normal Precautions." Its only named risk is natural disasters: earthquakes, tsunamis, typhoons, flooding and extreme heat. (as_of: 2025-11-24; source: https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories)
- Mainland China and Hong Kong were each at "Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution" and Macau at "Level 3: Reconsider Travel" in advisories dated 3 September 2026. (as_of: 2026-09-03; source: https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories)
- The China advisory cites arbitrary enforcement of local laws, exit bans and the risk of wrongful detention. It does not cite armed conflict. (as_of: 2026-09-03; source: https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories)
- On 9 Oct 2026 the State Department advisory feed had 223 entries: 85 Level 1, 86 Level 2, 27 Level 3 and 25 Level 4. Burma appears twice among the Level 4 entries. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories)
- Level 4 destinations on 9 Oct 2026 included Ukraine, Russia, Belarus, Iran, Lebanon, Syria, Gaza and North Korea. Japan, South Korea and the Philippines were at Level 1-2. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories)

## Resolution notes

- KXTAIWANLVL4 resolves Yes if the State Department issues, updates or reaffirms a Level 4 travel advisory for Taiwan after Issuance and before the strike date. The level must apply to the whole country, not one region. An advisory counts as issued when it appears on travel.state.gov with an official publication date. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/events/KXTAIWANLVL4?with_nested_markets=true)
- Advisories dated for future events must take effect before the strike date. Each market closes early once a qualifying advisory is issued. Under the 2020 pattern, a global health advisory that left Taiwan's own page at Level 1 would not obviously qualify.
