# Base Rate: Midterm Penalty

Back to [[Politics Knowledge Index]]

Scope: seats the president's party gains or loses in midterm House and Senate elections, and how often it loses the House majority.

## Claims

- The president's party lost House seats in 20 of the 22 midterms from 1938 on; the exceptions were 1998 (Clinton) and 2002 (G.W. Bush). (as_of: 2025-08-28; source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-history-tells-us-about-the-2026-midterm-elections)
- Across 1934-2018 midterms the president's party gained House seats only three times and Senate seats six times; correlation of approval with seat change was r = 0.61 for the House and r = 0.45 for the Senate. (as_of: 2022-08-30; source: https://presidency.ucsb.edu/analyses/the-2022-midterm-elections-what-the-historical-data-suggest)
- The president's party held the House going into 13 of the 20 midterms from 1946 to 2022 and lost it in 7 (1946, 1954, 1994, 2006, 2010, 2018, 2022); derived from the House Clerk party-division table. (as_of: 2023-01-03; source: https://history.house.gov/Institution/Party-Divisions/Party-Divisions/)
