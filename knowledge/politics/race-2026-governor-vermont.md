# Race: 2026 Vermont Governor

Back to [[Politics Knowledge Index]]

Gov. Phil Scott (R) seeks a sixth two-year term against Democrat Amanda Janoo, November 3, 2026.

## Claims

- In 2024 Scott won re-election with 73.43% to Democrat Esther Charlestin's 21.83%. (as_of: 2024-11-05; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Vermont_gubernatorial_election)
- Vermont and New Hampshire are the two Republican-held governorships on the 2026 ballot in states Harris won in 2024. Vermont is the only one Harris won by double digits. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Vermont_gubernatorial_election)
- Economist Amanda Janoo announced her campaign for governor. (as_of: 2026-03-10; source: https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/amanda-janoo-announces-bid-for-vermont-governor/)
- Scott won the Aug 11 Republican primary with 98.5% (21,466 votes). (as_of: 2026-08-11; source: https://electionresults.vermont.gov/)
- Janoo won the Aug 11 Democratic primary with 49.1% (39,614 votes) to Aly Richards's 46.6% (37,570). (as_of: 2026-08-11; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Vermont_gubernatorial_election)
- Also on the ballot: Brian Judd (independent), Dean Roy (Freedom and Unity) and June Goodband (Green Mountain Peace and Justice). (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Vermont_gubernatorial_election)
- UNH Survey Center, Jul 15–20, 954 LV: Scott 44, Janoo 33. (as_of: 2026-07-21; source: https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1981&context=survey_center_polls)
- UNH Survey Center, Sep 17–21, 829 LV: Janoo 49, Scott 43, Judd 2. (as_of: 2026-09-24; source: https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2005&context=survey_center_polls)
- VTDigger called the race the tightest Vermont governor's race in a decade. It cited Scott's vetoes of progressive bills as one reason he is losing crossover support. (as_of: 2026-09-25; source: https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/25/latest-poll-shows-gov-phil-scott-and-amanda-janoo-in-tightest-race-in-a-decade/)

- Janoo raised more than Scott in September, according to campaign finance disclosures. (as_of: 2026-10-02; source: https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/janoo-outraised-gov-scott-in-september-disclosures-show/)

## Resolution notes

- GOVPARTYVT-26-D: "If a representative of the Democratic party is inaugurated as the governor of Vermont pursuant to the 2026 election, then the market resolves to Yes." The R market uses the same wording for the Republican party. Both can be settled early once a consensus of media organizations projects the winner. Expected expiration is 2027-01-05. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/events/GOVPARTYVT-26?with_nested_markets=true)
- Vermont's constitution requires a majority of the popular vote. If no candidate gets one, the General Assembly chooses from the top three. The market keys on who is inaugurated, so in that case the legislature's vote decides it. Third-party candidates make a sub-50% winner possible. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Vermont)
- Precedent: in 2002 no candidate won a popular-vote majority, and the General Assembly elected Jim Douglas, the plurality winner. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Vermont_gubernatorial_election)
