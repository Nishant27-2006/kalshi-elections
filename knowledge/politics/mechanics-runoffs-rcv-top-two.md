# Mechanics: Runoffs, Ranked-Choice and Top-Two Systems

Back to [[Politics Knowledge Index]]

Scope: election systems that delay or reshape when a winner is known (GA majority runoff, LA closed primaries, CA/WA top-two, ME/AK ranked-choice), with dated historical examples.

## Claims

### Georgia majority runoff
- Georgia's SB 202, signed by Gov. Brian Kemp in March 2021, cut the gap between a general election and its runoff from nine weeks to four weeks. (as_of: 2022-12-10; source: https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2022/12/10/effect-of-georgias-voting-law-unclear-despite-high-turnout)
- Georgia's 2021 Senate runoffs were held January 5, 2021, and the AP called both races (Ossoff, Warnock) on January 6, 2021. (as_of: 2021-01-06; source: https://www.wrdw.com/2021/01/06/georgia-senate-runoffs-where-do-we-stand-as-votes-continue-to-come-in)
- In the November 8, 2022 Georgia Senate general, neither Warnock nor Walker passed 50%, so the race went to a December 6 runoff; Libertarian Chase Oliver took about 2.1%. (as_of: 2022-11-23; source: https://news.ballotpedia.org/2022/11/23/raphael-warnock-d-and-herschel-walker-r-are-running-in-the-runoff-for-u-s-senate-in-georgia/)
- Warnock defeated Walker in the December 6, 2022 runoff. (as_of: 2022-12-06; source: https://www.npr.org/2022/12/06/1141162415/democratic-sen-raphael-warnock-defeats-republican-herschel-walker-in-georgia-run)
- Georgia law requires a majority, not a plurality; in the May 2026 primary, State School Superintendent Richard Woods got 49.89% and was forced into a June 16 runoff. (as_of: 2026-05-22; source: https://thecurrentga.org/2026/05/22/take-two-these-democratic-republican-statewide-races-are-headed-to-june-runoffs/)
- Georgia's state portal says that if no candidate gets a majority (50 percent plus one) in the general election, the top two advance to a runoff, and that the 2026 general election runoff is December 1, 2026. (as_of: 2026-10-09; source: https://georgia.gov/node/34081)

### Louisiana: jungle primary to closed party primary
- Louisiana's Act 1 of the 2024 First Extraordinary Session (HB 17), signed by Gov. Jeff Landry on January 22, 2024, replaced the jungle primary with closed party primaries effective January 1, 2026, limited by the Senate to Congressional, U.S. Senate, Public Service Commission and BESE races. (as_of: 2025-03-22; source: https://sites.law.lsu.edu/ljsjp/?p=794)
- Louisiana held its first closed U.S. Senate primary on May 16, 2026; incumbent Bill Cassidy finished third (about 24%) behind Julia Letlow (45%) and John Fleming (28%). (as_of: 2026-05-17; source: https://www.wrkf.org/2026-05-17/incumbent-bill-cassidy-knocked-out-in-louisianas-gop-us-senate-primary)
- In the June 27, 2026 party runoffs, Letlow beat Fleming 57% to 43% for the Republican nomination and Jamie Davis beat Gary Crockett 80% to 20% for the Democratic nomination. (as_of: 2026-06-27; source: https://www.ktbs.com/news/arklatex-politics/louisiana-elects-u-s-senate-nominees-in-primary-runoff-election/article_e0c882ca-15d4-4a37-9c82-0096aa72d584.html)

### California and Washington top-two
- California voters approved Proposition 14 (top-two primary) in June 2010 with 54.2% of the vote. (as_of: 2010-06-10; source: https://www.jurist.org/news/2010/06/california-voters-opt-to-change-primary-election-system)
- The U.S. Supreme Court upheld Washington's top-two primary (Initiative 872) on March 18, 2008, in Washington State Grange v. Washington State Republican Party. (as_of: 2008-03-18; source: https://www.jurist.org/news/2008/03/go-supreme-court-rules-washington-state/)
- Xavier Becerra (D) and Steve Hilton (R) advanced from California's June 2, 2026 top-two primary for governor, out of 61 candidates; Ballotpedia reported the result on June 12. (as_of: 2026-06-12; source: https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/06/12/xavier-becerra-d-and-steve-hilton-r-advance-from-the-top-two-primary-for-governor-of-california/)

### Maine ranked-choice
- Maine uses ranked-choice voting in primaries and in general elections for federal offices (including president), but not in general elections for governor or the Legislature, because that would conflict with the Maine Constitution. (as_of: 2024-10-21; source: https://www.wbur.org/news/2024/10/21/how-does-maine-ranked-choice-voting-work)
- In ME-02 in 2018, Poliquin led the first round 46.4% to 45.5%; the ranked-choice tabulation on November 15, 2018 made Golden the winner, 50.53% to 49.47%. (as_of: 2018-11-15; source: https://bangordailynews.com/2018/11/15/politics/golden-defeats-poliquin-in-contested-2nd-district-ranked-choice-count/)
- Graham Platner won Maine's June 9, 2026 Democratic Senate primary. (as_of: 2026-06-10; source: https://www.wfmz.com/news/ap/ap-national/election-2026-maine-senate/image_29dcec7c-d596-5f8c-abf1-7938333c4368.html)

### Alaska top-four primary and ranked-choice general
- In Alaska's August 16, 2022 House special election (its first statewide RCV race), tabulation took place on August 31, 15 days later, and Peltola beat Palin 51.5% to 48.5% in the final round. (as_of: 2022-08-31; source: https://cfjctoday.com/2022/08/31/peltola-beats-palin-wins-alaska-house-special-election/)
- Alaska ran its ranked-choice tabulation for the November 8, 2022 general on November 23, 2022: Murkowski beat Tshibaka 135,972 to 117,299, and Peltola beat Palin 136,893 to 112,255. (as_of: 2022-11-23; source: https://www.adn.com/politics/2022/11/23/watch-live-at-4-pm-alaskas-ranked-choice-vote-tabulation-in-the-2022-election)
- Alaska's 2024 Ballot Measure 2, which would have repealed RCV and open primaries, led on election night but failed by 664 of 340,110 votes (50.1% No to 49.9% Yes) as absentee and late ballots were counted. (as_of: 2024-11-20; source: https://alaskapublic.org/elections/2024-11-20/alaskas-ranked-choice-repeal-measure-fails-by-664-votes)
- A recount requested by the Alaska Republican Party confirmed the repeal failed, by 743 votes. (as_of: 2024-12-09; source: https://alaskapublic.org/news/2024-12-10/the-recount-is-over-alaska-will-keep-ranked-choice-voting)
- A new initiative to repeal RCV and open primaries, Ballot Measure 2 (24ESEG), is on Alaska's November 2026 ballot. (as_of: 2026-07-29; source: https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-07-29/alaskans-to-vote-on-ballot-measure-that-would-overhaul-state-election-system)

## Resolution notes

A GA runoff or AK/ME RCV tabulation delays the media consensus, not the contract's rule.
