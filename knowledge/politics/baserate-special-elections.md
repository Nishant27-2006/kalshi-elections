# Base Rate: Special Elections

Back to [[Politics Knowledge Index]]

Average special-election swing against the prior presidential baseline, by cycle, and how well it has tracked the next national House vote.

## Claims

- In 46 special elections in 2018, the overall swing from the 2016 presidential result averaged 12 points toward Democrats. (as_of: 2018-08-07; source: https://election.princeton.edu/?p=926)
- The 2018 national House popular vote margin was 8.7 points (Democratic), matching the final generic-ballot average. (as_of: 2018-12-04; source: https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/17660988-how-fivethirtyeight-s-2018-midterm-forecasts-did)
- In 2023, Democrats won special elections by margins 11 points higher than the weighted relative partisanship of their districts. (as_of: 2023-09-20; source: https://abcnews.com/538/democrats-winning-big-special-elections/story?id=103315703)
- 538 (Rakich): in each of the past three election cycles, a party's average overperformance across all special elections in the cycle was a close match for the eventual House popular vote, give or take a couple of points; 1997-98 was an exception. (as_of: 2023-09-20; source: https://abcnews.com/538/democrats-winning-big-special-elections/story?id=103315703)
- Split Ticket: special elections in 2021 showed a swing to the right from 2020; summer-2022 post-Dobbs specials showed a 5-point swing to the left from 2020; 2023 Democrats were outperforming Biden's 2020 margins by 8 points. (as_of: 2023-09-27; source: https://split-ticket.org/2023/09/27/do-special-elections-mean-anything/)
- 538 (Rakich): from Jan. 1 to Sept. 19, 2023 Democrats overperformed by 11 points, but in specials from Nov. 7, 2023 onward Republicans overperformed by 1 point; the 2023-24 cycle average was a 6-point Democratic overperformance. (as_of: 2024-02-21; source: https://abcnews.com/538/2024-predictor-polls-special-elections/story?id=107369614)
- 538 (Rakich): special-election overperformance has virtually no correlation with the presidential popular vote; the relationship is with the House popular vote. (as_of: 2024-02-21; source: https://abcnews.com/538/2024-predictor-polls-special-elections/story?id=107369614)
- G. Elliott Morris: Democrats in 2025 special elections were running about 16 points ahead of Harris's 2024 margin, 5-6 points above the average Democratic overperformance in the 2017 cycle. (as_of: 2025-08-27; source: https://www.gelliottmorris.com/p/democrat-flips-trump-11-seat-in-iowa)
- Brookings: counting 2025 off-year wins in Virginia and New Jersey, 30 state legislative seats flipped Republican to Democratic, with zero flips the other way. (as_of: 2026-04-16; source: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-do-special-elections-mean-for-the-midterm-elections/)
- The Downballot: in a late-2017 window of about four months, Democrats overperformed the 2016 baseline by 25 points on average across 27 special elections; from about late March 2018 through the end of Trump's first term the average was about 4 points. (as_of: 2026-07-29; source: https://www.the-downballot.com/p/special-elections-can-tell-us-when)
- The Downballot: in roughly the first year of Biden's presidency Democrats underperformed the 2020 baseline by about 9 points; Democratic overperformance largely disappeared by the end of 2023. (as_of: 2026-07-29; source: https://www.the-downballot.com/p/special-elections-can-tell-us-when)
- The Downballot: across 110 special elections since January 2025, Democrats won 31 legislative flips (all of them) and overperformed Harris's 2024 margin by 12.5 points and Biden's 2020 margin by 8.4 points on average. (as_of: 2026-08-19; source: https://www.the-downballot.com/p/100-specials-since-trumps-return)
- The Downballot: the 2025-26 special-election data implies a Democratic environment of about 12 points, slightly better than the same data showed in 2018, though the 2018 House popular vote "fell a few points shy of that mark." (as_of: 2026-08-19; source: https://www.the-downballot.com/p/100-specials-since-trumps-return)

## Resolution notes

- Baselines differ by source: 538 uses weighted relative partisanship (district lean relative to the nation), while Downballot, Split Ticket, Morris and Princeton compare with the raw prior presidential margin. Do not mix the two without adjusting. (as_of: 2024-02-21; source: https://abcnews.com/538/2024-predictor-polls-special-elections/story?id=107369614)
- Overperformance shifts within a cycle (late 2017 vs. 2018; early vs. late 2023), so a running average early in a cycle can drift by the midterm. (as_of: 2026-07-29; source: https://www.the-downballot.com/p/special-elections-can-tell-us-when)
